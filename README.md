### 1.1 Real Numbers: Algebra Essentials

Real numbers are the combination of all rational and irrational numbers. Natural numbers make up the simplest form of numbers, the counting numbers. The numbers include 1, 2, 3... and so on. Other categories of rational numbers are negative numbers, the inverse of their positive number, and zero. The set of all rational numbers is described as m/n where m and n are integers, and n != 0. This shows that rational number are just fractions. Meaning they can be expressed as terminating or repeated decimals. The book uses the example to show why 7 is a rational number. It is be ause we can use the set of all rational nimbers and find two numbers that equate to 7 where n is not zero.
